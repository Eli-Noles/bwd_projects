The Gospel

God created us for His glory.

God made all of us in his own image so that we reflect his beauty and other attributes. He breathed life into us (Genesis 2:7), and gave us a perfect, original home (Genesis 2:9). And we have the option to bear His name, for His glory (Isaiah 43:7).

Every human should live for God’s glory.

Christians can (and should) live for the glory of God by loving him (Matthew 22:37), trusting him (Romans 4:20), being thankful to him (Psalm 50:23), obeying him (Matthew 5:16), and treasuring him above all things (Philippians 3:8; Matthew 10:37). When we do these things we bring about God’s glory. (1 Corinthians 10:31)

However, we have all sinned and fallen short of God’s glory.

None of us has loved, trusted or thanked or obeyed or treasured God as we should (Romans 1:21–23, 3:10-18, 3:23). In his righteous, holy, and just character, His wrath rests on us (Romans 1:18) and he condemns us to eternal punishment for both our grevous amount wicked and evil acts (Romans 1:29-32), and lack of righteous works (Matthew 25: 41-46, Romans 1:6-8).

Therefore we all deserve eternal punishment (Romans 6:23).

Those who do not obey the Lord Jesus “will suffer the punishment of eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of his might” (2 Thessalonians 1:9).

Yet, in his great mercy, God sent his only Son Jesus Christ into the world to provide for sinners the way of eternal life.

“God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life” (John 3:16). “Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us, because it is written, 'Cursed is everyone who is hung on a tree'(Deuteronomy 21:23)” (Galatians 3:13). “Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring us to God” (1 Peter 3:18).

Therefore eternal life is a free gift to all who will trust in Christ as Lord and Savior and Supreme Treasure of their lives.

“Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved” (Acts 16:31). “If you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved” (Romans 10:9). “By grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast” (Ephesians 2:8–9). “I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me” (Galatians 2:20). “I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things and count them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ” (Philippians 3:8).

The Church: A Body of Believers

The church is an essential place for the spiritual growth, fellowship, and encouragement of a believer. To clarify, the church doesn't consist mainly of the building it's in. To make the church building an article of God's presence is to fall into the same sin the Israelites did in (1 Samuel 4:1-10). Jesus laid out the first concept of the power of the church when he said, "For where two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there among them." (Matthew 18:20). Furthermore, Paul, in his letter to the church of Colossae (Colossians), told of the supremacy of Christ in the church, saying, "He is also the head of the body, the church; he is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, so that he might come to have first place everything" (Colossians 1:18). Paul also outlines what role we play in Christ's body, saying that even though we are (re)born of one Spirit, we all have different spiritual gifts and serve different roles in the church (1 Corinthians 12:12-30). In verse 31, he tells us to desire the greater gifts (see vs. 28), but he will show us "an even better way." provoke one another to good works and encourage each other, and we can't do this if we neglect to gather (Hebrews 10:24-25).
